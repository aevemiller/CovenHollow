# Bristle & Twig, Broom Wrights — Master Build Packet
### XPS foam board + lumber construction · mono-pitch trade workshop · ~3/4 scale · print me for the garage

**Finished size:** 60" wide × 48" deep · front wall 66" falling to back wall 50" · stove pipe to ~84"
**System:** four hinged wall panels (pin-hinge corners, same as the bakery) plus ONE mono-pitch roof panel — the easiest collapse in the village: two L-pairs and a single flat.
**Companion:** the interactive 3D model (explode it, swing the doors, hover any part for its cut size).
**Chimney Rule:** the stove pipe counts as a chimney, so it ships smoke-ready: hollow 4" pipe, ground space behind the back wall for a fog machine in its weather box, cord path to the West zone, restricted top. No exceptions, even for a pipe the width of a coffee can.

## Part 3 — Step-by-Step (six sessions)

### Session 1 — Wall frames (3 hrs)
1. Build A and B flat: plates, studs, rails, door framing in A, gussets everywhere. The trapezoid sides C and D need the ~18° angle on their rake rail ends; set your saw once and cut both so they match.
2. Screw the stove pipe mast to B's frame, top 12" proud.

### Session 2 — Skins (2–3 hrs)
3. Skin all four panels flat, washer screws every 12". Cut the door opening in A. Trim C and D's skins to the trapezoid against the rake rails.
4. Roof panel: frame, skin, 3" overhangs all around, fascia strip glued to the tall edge.

### Session 3 — Carve and paint (2 hrs + dry)
5. Clapboard scoring on all walls (4.5" courses), wire-brush grain, knots at random.
6. Timber recipe: dark base, gray dry-brush, cream dry-brush, wash in the scores. Doors and trim in dark green, two coats. Fascia in dark green ready for lettering.

### Session 4 — Doors and signs (2–3 hrs)
7. Build the two door leaves, Z-bracing on the outer faces, plank-score and paint green. Hang on their loose-pin hinges, mount the cabin hooks so each leaf pins open flat against its side wall.
8. Letter the fascia: "BRISTLE AND TWIG" with "BROOM WRIGHTS" beneath, gold pen over guide lines.
9. The trade sign: cut the board, build the broom in relief (handle strip half-inch proud, bristle fan carved with the ballpoint and knife), gold frame line, black bracket arm on the front corner, hang it on the S-hooks. It should move in a breeze; that movement is the oldest shop advertisement there is.

### Session 5 — Assembly (2 hrs, two people)
10. Pin-hinge the box, bakery-style: fixed hinges at two corners, loose-pin at the two diagonals, three per corner, every leaf numbered.
11. Roof panel on: it slopes front-down-to-back, hook-and-eyes to both plates. One panel, no ridge, no piano hinge. Enjoy how easy this one is.
12. Stove pipe: duct over its mast, elbow at the base pointing through a 4" hole in the back skin, black paint. Fog machine in its weather box on the ground behind, short duct run in, cycle timer set stingy (5 seconds every two minutes) because a workshop stove simmers, it doesn't bake.

### Session 6 — The shop opens (2 hrs)
13. Interior: workbench against the back wall, vise clamped on, half-bound broom in the vise with twine hanging loose, tool wall (real rusty tools on nails, or black foam silhouettes), twine spool shelf, shavings scattered thick, lantern hung from the roof frame on a hook.
14. Exterior: broom stock rack leaned by the doors (two rails + rungs, brooms priced in pence, one SOLD tag), bristle sheaves against the wall (your real grass bundles, finally in their canon role), the repair queue with claim tickets, the fitting post by the door.
15. Site day: four stakes, two sandbags inside, doors pinned open, citation on the parking rail, crashed broom in the tree. Open for business since 1692.

## Part 4 — Field Notes for This Build
The doors are the personality; spend your patience there, since they're the largest green surfaces in the village and everyone touches distressed doors with their eyes. The single roof panel makes this the fastest teardown you own: pull two pin sets, fold, lift one flat. On any real wind warning, the doors come off their pin hinges in under a minute and lie inside. And the stove pipe's stingy smoke timer matters, because the bakery's chimney says "baking day" while this pipe should only ever say "the kettle's on."
