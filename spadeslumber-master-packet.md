# Spade & Slumber — Master Build Packet
### (the cemetery keeper's shed, formerly "the sexton's") · XPS + lumber · open-front lean-to · ~3/4 scale

**Finished size:** 40" wide × 24" deep · front 54" falling to 42" back · open front bay, no doors
**System:** four pin-hinge wall panels + one mono-pitch roof panel: Bristle & Twig's little sibling, the same fold at half the size.
**Companion:** the interactive 3D model (explode it, hover any part for its cut size).
**Chimney Rule:** no chimney on this build. The only smoke at Spade & Slumber is the ground fog crawling past it, which comes from the cemetery's fog chiller, not from the shed.
**Name alternates, if Spade & Slumber doesn't sing for you:** The Last Nap Lodge, or Dig & Dream Groundskeeping. All follow the village's trade-sign naming (Cackle Crust, Bristle & Twig).

### Paint
The timber recipe for the shed (dark base, gray, cream, wash). For headstones: charcoal wash over gray base, two lighter gray dry-brushes, moss green stippled in crevices and along one shoulder only (weather hits one side; keep your moss consistent to the yard's north). Matte seal everything.

**Budget:** roughly $70–110. **Time:** three sessions plus headstone evenings. The easiest structure in the village.

## Part 3 — Step-by-Step (three sessions)

### Session 1 — Frames and skins (3 hrs)
1. Build all five panels flat (four walls + roof), gussets at every joint, the two side rakes cut at the same saw setting so they match.
2. Skin everything, cut the open bay in the front panel, clapboard-score, wire-brush the grain.

### Session 2 — Paint and sign (2 hrs + dry)
3. Timber recipe on all faces. The bay's inside edges get the dark base only, so the opening reads as shadow.
4. Fascia sign: "SPADE & SLUMBER" in gold over guide lines, with "lawn & lasting care" small beneath. Hang the lantern hook inside the bay's header.

### Session 3 — Assembly and the corner comes alive (2 hrs)
5. Pin-hinge the box (fixed corners and loose-pin diagonals, three each, leaves numbered), roof panel hooked on, four stakes.
6. Hang the tools in the bay on nails: shovel, rake, pitchfork, coil of rope. Lantern lit. This shed has no door because its whole interior is the display.
7. The fresh dig: tarp down, topsoil mounded, shovel standing in it, gloves on the nearest headstone, lunch pail on the crate. Wheelbarrow parked somewhere it wasn't yesterday.
8. Plant the headstones in an unplanned scatter (real graveyards don't grid), blanks leaned on the shed, ground-fog chiller hidden behind the fence line per Upgrade Sheet U4's sibling notes.

## Part 4 — Field Notes
Open-front buildings live or die by interior contrast: the inside must be darker than the outside so the lantern and tools glow out of shadow, which is why the bay's inner edges stay unpainted dark. The headstone scatter should look inherited, not installed; set them with different leans (a stone that leans reads as eighty years old, free). And resist roofing this one with shingles: a lean-to tool shed wants plain boards, which is one less step and more correct.
