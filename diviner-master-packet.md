# Diviner's Tower — Master Build Packet
### XPS foam board + lumber construction · hexagonal observatory · ~3/4 scale · print me for the garage

**Finished size:** 36" across the flats (six facets, each ~20¾" wide) · walls 66" · cone peak 96" · crescent finial to ~102"
**System:** six framed facet panels hinged into a hexagon. Two loose-pin corners split it into two 3-facet shells that accordion flat. The cone lifts off whole.
**Companion:** the interactive 3D model (orbit it, split the shells, hide the foam to see the frame).
**Chimney Rule note:** no chimney on this build, so no smoke provision needed. Her magic doesn't burn wood.

## Part 2 — Cut Schedule

### The six facet frames (all identical skeletons)
| Piece | Cut | Qty per facet |
|---|---|---|
| Uprights | 2×2 @ 63" | 2 |
| Top + bottom rails | 2×2 @ 17¾" (fits between uprights) | 2 |
| Mid rail | 2×2 @ 17¾" at 33" height | 1 |
| XPS skin | 20¾" × 66" | 1 (two per half sheet-width; ~3 sheets total) |

**The angle trick:** the skins, not the lumber, make the hexagon angle. Cut each skin's two long edges with your knife tilted to a 30° bevel (mark 3/8" back on the inside face and cut to it). When two beveled skins meet, they close the 120° corner cleanly and the hinges hide inside. Test the bevel on scrap until two offcuts mate flat.

### The three specialty facets
| Facet | Opening | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Door facet (faces the green) | Arched opening 17" wide × 56" tall, arch springs at 42" | Frame the sides with the uprights themselves; the arch is cut in the skin only, reinforced behind with a plywood strip. Curtain dowel inside at 57". |
| Telescope facet (45–90° off the door, aimed at open sky) | 12" × 10" opening, sill at 50" | 2×2 blocking above and below. Two XPS shutters 6" × 11" glued open at angles. Telescope pokes through on a dowel pivot screwed to the mid rail so you can angle it skyward. |
| Dial facet (street-visible side) | none | Mounts the moon dial: a 14" XPS disc, ½" proud, gold rings and a crescent, plus "FORTUNES & FORECASTS" arched beneath in gold pen. |

### The cone
| Piece | Cut | Qty |
|---|---|---|
| Spider frame | 2×2 hub post @ 30" + six 2×2 spokes @ ~20" from hub base to wall-plate corners | 1 |
| Cone panels | XPS triangles, 24" base × 37" slant (cut one, check fit, then batch the rest) | 6 |
| Finial | Two 10" XPS crescents laminated on the dowel mast | 1 |

Panels glue to the spokes edge-to-edge (foam-safe adhesive plus painter's tape while curing); seams get a thin bead of foam filler carved smooth. The whole cone lifts off as one piece and latches down with the hook-and-eyes.

## Part 3 — Step-by-Step (seven sessions)

### Session 1 — Facet frames (3 hrs)
1. Cut all frame lumber. Build six identical rectangles on the flat: uprights, top, bottom, and mid rails, gussets at every joint, square checked by diagonals.
2. Add the telescope blocking to one frame and mark one frame DOOR and one DIAL with the paint pen. Label every frame 1 through 6 now; the hexagon only closes if they go together in the order you hinged them.

### Session 2 — Skins and bevels (3 hrs)
3. Cut six skins at 20¾" × 66". Bevel both long edges of each at ~30° (knife against a straightedge, tilted; practice on scrap first).
4. Cut the door arch in its skin and the telescope opening in its. Glue and washer-screw all skins to their frames.

### Session 3 — Carving and paint (2 hrs + dry time)
5. No clapboard on this one; the tower is smooth plaster. Rough the surface lightly with a sanding block so it reads as aged stucco, and score a few fine cracks radiating from corners with the ballpoint.
6. Paint: midnight blue on facets 1, 3, 5 and near-black navy on 2, 4, 6; the alternation gives the tower depth at night. Cone panels get the near-black. Two coats.

### Session 4 — The sky (2 hrs, the fun one)
7. Stars in gold pen: sparse at the bottom, denser toward the top, mixing three sizes (dots, four-point stars, and six tiny constellations you invent). The density gradient is what makes the tower read as rising into the night.
8. The moon dial: gold rings, moon phases around the rim, a crescent at center, mounted ½" proud on its facet. Letter "FORTUNES & FORECASTS" beneath, guide lines first, always.

### Session 5 — Hinging the hexagon (2 hrs, two people)
9. Stand facets 1-2-3 in a shallow arc on flat ground. Hinge the two corners between them (fixed hinges, leaves inside, 3 per corner). That's shell A. Repeat with 4-5-6 for shell B.
10. Stand both shells together into the hexagon and join the two remaining corners with the loose-pin hinges. Check every corner closes to 120° and the top plates make a level ring. Number the pin hinges.
11. Practice the fold once: pull two pin sets, each shell accordions flat, the two flats stack. Reassembly should take one person ten minutes.

### Session 6 — Cone and finial (2–3 hrs)
12. Build the spider on the assembled tower so it fits YOUR hexagon, not the theoretical one: hub post centered, six spokes to the corners, screwed and gusseted.
13. Glue cone panels to the spokes, tape seams until cured, fill and smooth. Mount the crescent finial on its dowel through the hub.
14. Lift the cone off, paint it on sawhorses (near-black, sparse stars), seat it back on, and mark and mount the six hook-and-eyes.

### Session 7 — The observatory comes alive (2 hrs)
15. Telescope: PVC on its dowel pivot through the shutter opening, angled up 30–40°, brass-painted (over primer), mirror glint in the sky end. Shutters glued open around it.
16. Doorway: curtain dowel in, velvet hung with a narrow center gap, the color-shifting puck on the floor inside washing up the curtains, crystal ball on its draped stool catching the glow just inside the gap.
17. Fairy lights behind the telescope shutter on a timer, so the observatory reads as in-use.
18. Site day: four rebar stakes through alternating base rails, sandbag inside, and her forecast pinned to the community board: "Tonight: full dark, waxing dread, 100% chance of spirits."

## Part 4 — Field Notes for This Build
The bevel is the build; get the 30° edge right on scrap before touching real skins. Hexagons shrug wind (no flat face square to a gust), so four stakes and one sandbag genuinely suffice. The violet doorway is the village's only sanctioned cool light, so resist adding more; contrast is what makes it read as magic. And paint the gold stars LAST, after sealing everything else, because gold pen over matte sealer stays crisp while sealer over gold pen dulls it.
