# Owl Post — Master Build Packet
### XPS foam board + lumber construction · post office kiosk · ~3/4 scale · print me for the garage

**Finished size:** 30" × 30" footprint · walls 56" · ridge 78" · raven on top (not included in height, per ordinance)
**System:** one rigid box (no fold system — it moves whole, and the steep roof lifts off as a lid). The cubby unit is separate furniture that slides in from the front, and the rear panel opens for restocking and letter collection.
**Companion:** the interactive 3D model (lift the lid, slide the cubby unit out, open the back).
**Chimney Rule:** no chimney. The mail moves by wing, not by fire.

## Part 3 — Step-by-Step (five sessions)

### Session 1 — The box frame (2–3 hrs)
1. Build two identical side ladders: two 56" posts joined by 27" rails at top and bottom. Gusset the corners.
2. Stand the ladders and join them with the remaining 27" rails front and back, top and bottom, plus the front mid rails at 18" and 48" (these frame the cubby opening). Check square by diagonals at the top ring.

### Session 2 — Skins and openings (2 hrs)
3. Glue and washer-screw the four skins on. Cut the cubby opening (26 × 30 between its rails), the slot opening low on the front, and the 24 × 40 rear opening, saving the rear cutout to become the door.
4. Hang the rear panel on its 2 hinges with a hook latch. Score all four faces as clapboard (4.5" courses) except the front below the slot, which scores as recessed paneling for the dark green stallriser look.

### Session 3 — Paint and gilt (2 hrs + dry)
5. Timber recipe on the clapboard: dark base, gray dry-brush, cream dry-brush, wash in the scores. Dark green on the paneling, rear door, and trim edges.
6. The sign: XPS board ~26" × 8", dark green, gold-pen "OWL POST" with "NIGHT DELIVERY ONLY" beneath, guide lines first. Mounts to the front gable once the lid exists.

### Session 4 — The lid (2–3 hrs)
7. Frame the lid on the ground: ridge beam, four steep rafters down to the eave rails, gussets. It should sit over the wall tops like a shoebox lid, on small locating blocks glued inside the eave rails.
8. Skin the gables and roof planes, shingle bottom-up with staggered joints, cap the ridge. Glue the perch socket at the ridge front. Mount the sign on the front gable. Set the lid on and place the four hook-and-eyes.

### Session 5 — The post office opens (2 hrs)
9. Slide the cubby unit in through the front opening; it rests on the 18" rail and a cleat inside the back wall. Brass label frame under each cubby, names on tea-stained card stock (pencil until the witches are christened, then gold ink).
10. Mount the brass slot through the front skin with its ledge board beneath; set the bin inside under it. Post the placard: "LETTERS TO THE RESIDENTS OF COVEN HOLLOW. Replies delivered by raven before next All Hallows."
11. Stock the cubbies with wax-sealed letter bundles, leave one glowing (LED puck taped behind it, wire down the inside corner), one stuffed to overflowing, one empty with the name plate missing: a vacancy, or an eviction. Stack "FRAGILE: HEXED" parcels outside.
12. Site day: raven pinned into its socket, lantern lit on the eave, four stakes, sandbag inside, hours placard: "Night delivery only. Closed during full moons (staff molting)."

## Part 4 — Field Notes for This Build
This is the village's best warm-up build: every technique the bakery needs, at a quarter the size and cost. The slot must actually work, so test an envelope through it before paint. Wire the glowing cubby before the unit slides in, not after. And buy or thrift the cubby unit if you find one, because vintage always beats foam where small hands will point from two feet away.
