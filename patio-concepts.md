# Four Patio Concepts — See Them Before Choosing

The feast failed because it changed furniture, not architecture. Each concept below changes three things: what hangs **overhead**, what you **cross** at the steps, and what stands against the **brick wall**. That's what makes your patio stop reading as your patio. Generate all four, pick the world you want.

---

## A. The Drying Yard & Still-Works

**In plain terms:** the patio becomes the apothecary's work yard. Overhead, a rope grid hung thick with drying herb bundles, so you look up into a ceiling of plants. Along the brick wall, shelf flats crowded with jars and a copper still (cardboard tubes and a pot, painted copper) with tubing that glows faintly amber. On a bench, bottles mid-filling with a funnel left in one. It smells like the cinnamon brooms you'll hide in it. The berm below is the garden that grows all this, so the geography tells one story.

## B. The Broom Docks

**In plain terms:** your patio is raised, and the village treats that literally: it's the broom landing platform. A big chalkboard departures board on the wall (DEPARTURES: MORTAL WORLD — CANCELLED. BOG OF REGRET — DELAYED, FOG.), broom racks with numbered berths, battered luggage trunks stacked and labeled, tall pole lanterns like runway lights, a windsock, and a landing circle painted on a canvas floor cloth. One broom mid-"landing" on wires. Kids climb the steps onto an airfield for witches. It connects to Bristle & Twig (someone builds the brooms), the parking rail (short-stay), and the crashed broom in the tree (a landing gone wrong).

## C. The Night Bazaar

**In plain terms:** two or three small vendor stalls, each a light PVC frame wearing a patched canvas awning, set up on the patio like traveling witch merchants pitched for the season. One sells charms and bones, one sells lanterns and candles, one is shuttered with a "back at moonrise" sign. Lantern strings zig-zag overhead. The stalls stand in front of your furniture and swallow it. This is the "market" energy but nomadic and mysterious where Coven Market is settled and cheerful.

## D. The Coven Hall

**In plain terms:** the open-air meeting hall where the village governs itself. Tall banners with hand-painted sigils hang from poles along both brick wings, a candle chandelier (the PVC drip candle recipe on a wagon-wheel or hoop) hangs overhead, benches face a lectern with an enormous open ledger, and a painted floor cloth puts a faded ceremonial circle underfoot. The meeting is clearly mid-session and abandoned: gavel down, cloaks over bench backs. This is the most theatrical option and the best stage for your community-board storyline.
