# Cackle Crust Bakery — Master Build Packet
### XPS foam board + lumber construction · ~3/4 scale · four-sided · collapsible · print me for the garage

**Finished size:** 72" wide × 60" deep · walls 66" · ridge 84" · chimney pots to ~112"
**System:** six framed panels (front gable, back gable, 2 sides, 2 roof halves) joined by hinges. Pins out = flat stack.
**Companion:** the interactive 3D model (orbit it, explode it, hide the foam to see the frame).

## Part 3 — Step-by-Step (ten sessions)

### Session 1 — Templates & frames, part one (3–4 hrs)
1. Snap a chalk full-size outline of the front gable on the driveway: 72" base, verticals to 66", peak at center 84". This chalk drawing is your assembly jig for both gables.
2. Cut all Panel A lumber per the schedule. Lay pieces on the chalk lines, check square (measure both diagonals of the rectangle portion — equal means square).
3. Screw joints with 2.5" screws, then glue-and-screw a plywood gusset across every joint with 1-5/8" screws. Gussets go on what will be the **inside** face.
4. Frame the serving opening (rough opening 30" wide, sill blocking top edge at 34", header bottom edge at 58") and display opening (30" wide, sill at 26", header at 54").
5. Repeat for Panel B on the same chalk lines, framing the door opening instead.

### Session 2 — Frames, part two (2–3 hrs)
6. Build both side panels and both roof panels flat. Gusset everything. Label every panel and every mating edge with a paint pen (A-left, C-front, etc.). Future-you assembles this in the dark in October; present-you leaves notes.

### Session 3 — Skinning (3–4 hrs)
7. Lay Panel A frame flat, best face up. Run beads of PL300 along every frame member.
8. Lay the 48×84 XPS sheet on, then the 24×84 partner. Seat them, then screw through foam into frame with 1-5/8" screws **through fender washers**, about every 12" along members. Snug, don't crush.
9. Trim skin flush to the gable rakes with a long knife against the frame.
10. Cut the two window openings and the door opening (Panel B) from the back with the knife, using the framing as your guide.
11. Skin the remaining panels the same way. Roof skins overhang their frames 3" at eaves and gable ends.

### Session 4 — Carving day (2–3 hrs, the fun one)
12. Draw clapboard courses every 4.5" with a straightedge; score each line firmly with a ballpoint.
13. Drag a wire brush lengthwise for grain; add knots with a pencil tip. Vary pressure; boards aren't twins.
14. Score the stallriser panels (two XPS rectangles, 30×22-ish) as recessed frames; score chimney skin (thin XPS to wrap the tube) in running-bond brick, courses 2.5" tall.

### Session 5 — Paint shop, base layers (2 hrs + dry time)
15. Base coat every carved surface black or raw umber, brushed into the scores. XPS takes latex directly, no primer needed. (Never bare-foam spray paint.)
16. Dry-brush gray across the high surfaces, almost-dry brush, long light strokes.
17. Second dry-brush cream, lighter still, catching only ridges. Water-thin dark wash into scores last. This four-layer stack is the whole wood illusion.

### Session 6 — Shopfront joinery (3 hrs)
18. Pilasters: 1" XPS strips 5" wide, glued proud of the wall at both front corners and between the bays; molding caps hot-glued on; paint dark green.
19. Stallrisers under both bays, dark green with the recessed-panel scoring.
20. Cornice molding above the bays; the gable sign board (XPS, 42×10") gets dark green field, pencil guide lines, then gold-pen "CACKLE CRUST BAKERY" in your best Victorian serif. Slow letters beat fast letters.
21. Bolt the 1×8 counter to its blocking through the serving opening at 34". Build the fixed "raised sash" above it from lattice muntins (2 over 3 panes) with parchment backing.
22. Display window: PET sheet hot-glued behind the opening, lattice muntin grid (9 panes) glued to its face, two shallow XPS shelves behind at pane heights.

### Session 7 — First assembly (2 hrs, two people)
23. Stand Panel A and Panel C on flat ground at right angles. Screw three fixed hinges up that corner (top, middle, bottom), leaves on the inside faces.
24. Continue around: C-to-B corner gets loose-pin hinges, B-to-D fixed, D-to-A loose-pin. Check the box for square, then number every hinge leaf pair.
25. Pull the two pin sets and practice the fold once: two L-pairs, flat. Reassemble. Twenty minutes from now on, forever.

### Session 8 — Roof & ridge (2 hrs)
26. Join roof panels E and F along the ridge with the piano hinge (barrel on the outside, so the fold closes shingle-to-shingle).
27. Two people lift the folded roof on, open it over the gables, center the overhangs. Mark and mount the 8 hook-and-eyes frame-to-plate.
28. Glue on shingle strip courses bottom-up, 3" exposure, staggered joints, with a sag carved into the eave course. Ridge cap strip over the hinge line (glued to ONE side only, so it folds).

### Session 9 — Chimney & rig (2–3 hrs)
29. Sleeve the form tube over Panel D's mast; screw through tube into mast.
30. Wrap with the brick-scored XPS skin, seams at the back; brick-red base, charcoal wash in the mortar lines, gray dry-brush. Great Stuff a mortar drip or two; carve back after cure.
31. Terracotta pots on the cap (two 4" tubes, 8" tall), gap between them left open over the duct exit.
32. Rig: fog machine in its vented bin at the base, duct up the tube interior, PC fan zip-tied just under the pots blowing gently up, machine + fan into the cycle timer. Amber spotlight staked behind the chimney aimed up through the exit. Test at dusk: adjust the timer until it puffs like someone's baking, not like something's burning.

### Session 10 — Site day (October)
33. Position per the map (east side, angled 30–45° to the street sightline). Rebar through the base-plate holes at all four corners. Sandbags inside against the side walls. Hooks latched. Shake test at the ridge.
34. Interior: stand the bread rack against the back wall and stock it (12–15 loaves, one gap where something sold), amber LED strip along a roof rail aimed down at the rack, apron on its hook, scale, ledger, counter bell. The rack is what the serving window exists to frame.
35. Exterior: display board on its trestles, bread baskets, Black Cauldron Milling sacks by the staff door, A-board chalked, cinnamon broom hidden behind the sacks, lantern lit.
36. Walk to the sidewalk at full dark and admire it. You built a building.

## Part 4 — XPS Field Notes (tape to the wall)
Foam-safe adhesive only; test every new product on scrap first. Cut XPS with a knife and straightedge (score deep, snap clean) — save the hot knife for curves, outdoors, standing upwind. Every screw through foam gets a fender washer. Latex paint straight onto XPS is fine; solvent sprays on bare foam dig craters. Score with a ballpoint before painting, never after. Seal everything matte before October. Store panels flat, off concrete, out of sun — XPS is UV-shy until painted.
